# 2006 Winter Olympics (Turin)

**Category**: Arts & Culture
**Key figures**: Shizuka Arakawa (figure skating); Cindy Klassen (speed skating); Evgeni Plushenko (figure skating); Michael Greis (biathlon); Ahn Hyun-soo and Jin Sun-yu (short-track speed skating)

## Summary

The 2006 Winter Olympics, officially the XX Olympic Winter Games, were held in Turin, Italy, from February 10 to 26, 2006. Some 2,508 athletes from 80 National Olympic Committees competed in 84 events across 15 disciplines grouped into 7 sports, with Albania, Ethiopia, and Madagascar making their Winter Olympic debuts. It was Italy's second time hosting the Winter Games, after Cortina d'Ampezzo in 1956, and the events were staged across venues in Turin and the surrounding Alpine valleys of Piedmont.

Germany topped the medal table for the third consecutive Winter Games, winning 11 gold medals and 29 medals overall. The United States finished second with 9 golds and 25 total medals, and host-region rival Austria third with 9 golds and 23 total. Canadian speed skater Cindy Klassen was the most decorated athlete of the Games with five medals (one gold, two silver, two bronze), while biathlete Michael Greis of Germany and South Korean short-track skaters Ahn Hyun-soo and Jin Sun-yu each won three golds. In figure skating, Shizuka Arakawa won Japan's first Olympic figure-skating gold medal, and Russia's Evgeni Plushenko took the men's title after setting a world-record short-program score. Canada's Duff Gibson, winning the skeleton event at age 39, became the oldest individual gold medalist in Winter Olympic history to that point.

The Games were overshadowed by a doping scandal centered on the Austrian cross-country skiing and biathlon teams. On February 18, 2006, Italian police raided the Austrian team's lodgings after learning that banned former coach Walter Mayer was present in violation of an existing ban, seizing blood-transfusion equipment and other doping paraphernalia. Immediate drug tests on the athletes returned negative, but the International Olympic Committee pursued the case: six Austrian athletes—biathletes Wolfgang Perner and Wolfgang Rottmann and cross-country skiers Martin Tauber, Jürgen Pinter, Johannes Eder, and Roland Diethart—were subsequently banned, and the Austrian Olympic Committee was fined US$1 million.

## Significance

As one of the largest recurring global sporting events, the Turin Games drew a worldwide television audience and stood alongside that summer's FIFA World Cup as one of 2006's two marquee international sporting spectacles hosted by Italy. Arakawa's victory was a landmark for Japanese winter sport, and the dominance of German and Austrian athletes in the traditional Alpine and Nordic disciplines reaffirmed central Europe's strength in winter competition.

The Austrian doping raid was among the most consequential Olympic anti-doping actions of its era, demonstrating a new willingness by host-nation authorities and the IOC to use police powers against organized doping and foreshadowing the more aggressive, intelligence-led enforcement that would reshape international sport in the following decade. The Games were also marked by substantial cost overruns, part of a broader early-21st-century pattern of escalating Olympic hosting expenses that would later prompt reforms to the bidding and staging process.
