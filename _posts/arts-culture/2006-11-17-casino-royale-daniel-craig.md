## Summary

*Casino Royale*, released on **November 17, 2006** in the United States (and on October 31 in the UK), is the twenty-first film in Eon Productions' James Bond series and the first to star **Daniel Craig** as MI6 agent James Bond. Directed by **Martin Campbell** (who had previously directed *GoldenEye* in 1995), the film adapts **Ian Fleming's 1953 debut Bond novel** — the third such adaptation after the 1954 CBS television version and the 1967 Columbia spoof — and depicts a younger, pre-hardened Bond earning his 00 status for the first time. Produced on a budget of approximately **$150 million**, it grossed approximately **$599.7 million worldwide**, becoming the highest-grossing Bond film to that date and the **fourth-highest-grossing film of 2006 globally**.

## The Casting Controversy

### Craig's Selection

Daniel Craig's casting as the sixth official Bond actor was announced in a press conference held at HMS Belfast, moored on the Thames in London, on **October 14, 2005**. The choice generated an unprecedented wave of public resistance. Online petitions, including the campaign at *danielcraigisnotbond.com*, collected tens of thousands of signatures. Critics argued that Craig — blond, stocky, and 5 ft 10 in (1.78 m) — violated Fleming's physical description of Bond as "tall, dark, and spare." Tabloids mocked him as "James Bland" and published unflattering paparazzi photographs of him in a life jacket taken during the HMS Belfast event. Several British newspapers ran readers' polls showing majority opposition to the casting.

Craig, born on **March 2, 1968** in Chester, Cheshire, had built a career in independent film and British television before the Bond role. Notable prior credits included *Some Voices* (2000), *Road to Perdition* (2002) alongside Tom Hanks, *Layer Cake* (2004) — the film that brought him to producer Barbara Broccoli's attention — and *Munich* (2005) directed by Steven Spielberg. Broccoli and co-producer Michael G. Wilson defended the choice specifically on the basis of Craig's physical and dramatic authority in *Layer Cake*, which they screened for MGM studio executives to make the case for the unconventional selection.

## Production

### The Reboot Decision

The decision to reboot the franchise was driven by the critical reception of *Die Another Day* (2002), the twentieth Bond film starring Pierce Brosnan, which had been widely mocked for its over-the-top CGI and implausible gadgets (including an invisible car and a space-based solar weapon). The film was a box-office success — grossing $431 million worldwide — but Eon Productions and EON/MGM agreed that the franchise had drifted toward self-parody. Rather than continue Brosnan's continuity, they opted to discard 40 years of accumulated mythology and begin from scratch with the first Bond adventure.

For the screenplay, producers commissioned **Neal Purvis** and **Robert Wade** — who had written the previous four Bond scripts — and brought in **Paul Haggis**, the *Crash* (2004) writer-director who had just won two Academy Awards, to revise the final draft. Haggis rewrote the film's ending and sharpened the emotional arc of Bond's relationship with Vesper Lynd. The screenplay stripped away gadgets, Q Branch, and the established trappings of the franchise, focusing instead on Bond's psychology and the emotional cost of his profession.

### Filming Locations

Principal photography began on **January 31, 2006**, in the Czech Republic, with casino scenes filmed at the Grand Hotel Pupp in Karlovy Vary (standing in for the fictional Casino Royale in Montenegro). Additional locations included:

- **Lake Como, Italy** — Villa La Gaeta, standing in for the home of villain Mr. White
- **The Bahamas** — Nassau and Paradise Island, for the opening section
- **Venice, Italy** — Canal and building interiors and exteriors for the film's climax
- **Montenegro** — establishing shots
- **Pinewood Studios**, Buckinghamshire, England — studio sequences, including the extended poker game constructed on a purpose-built set

The **opening parkour chase sequence** in Madagascar was filmed at locations in the Bahamas substituting for Africa, with the sequence at the construction crane site filmed at a real high-rise under construction in **Nassau**. The sequence, choreographed in consultation with *B13* (2004) parkour pioneer **Sébastien Foucan** — who plays the fleeing bomber — became one of the film's most-cited sequences for visceral physical stunt work.

### The Theme and Score

The title song **"You Know My Name"** was written and performed by **Chris Cornell**, the Soundgarden frontman. It replaced the traditional Bond-theme orchestral opening with a raw rock song that matched the film's harder-edged tone. The film's score was composed by **David Arnold**, who had scored the preceding five Bond films since *Tomorrow Never Dies* (1997).

## Box Office and Critical Reception

### Commercial Performance

*Casino Royale* opened in the United Kingdom on **October 31, 2006**, before its U.S. release three weeks later. Its U.K. opening weekend of £13.4 million ($24.8 million) set a new UK opening record at the time. The North American opening weekend of **$40.8 million** placed it at #1 at the domestic box office; the film spent three consecutive weekends at #1 in the U.S.

### Critical Response

The film holds a **95% approval rating on Rotten Tomatoes** (one of the highest scores for any major franchise blockbuster) and a Metacritic score of **80 out of 100**. Critics praised Craig's physically commanding, emotionally raw portrayal, which contrasted sharply with the lighter touch of the Brosnan era. *Empire* magazine called it "the best Bond film since *For Your Eyes Only*"; Roger Ebert gave it three-and-a-half stars, praising its return to "the thriller genre Bond was invented to inhabit."

### Awards

At the **BAFTA Awards 2007**, *Casino Royale* received a nomination for Outstanding British Film, and Craig was nominated for BAFTA Leading Actor. The **Evening Standard British Film Awards** named it Film of the Year. The film was also submitted as the United Kingdom's entry for the **Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film** category, though it was not shortlisted.

## Significance

*Casino Royale* reinvented a franchise that had grown formulaic, replacing the gadgetry and irony of preceding installments with psychological realism and physical grit. Craig's Bond was emotionally vulnerable, physically brutal, and morally compromised — qualities absent from the franchise since its earliest films. The film's influence on blockbuster cinema extended beyond Bond: it demonstrated that audiences would accept a rebooted franchise stripped of its inherited mythology if the emotional core was strong, a lesson applied to *Batman Begins* (2005) by Christopher Nolan and reinforced in later superhero reboots including *The Amazing Spider-Man* (2012) and *Man of Steel* (2013).

The film also marked a turning point in how studios managed established intellectual property. Rather than continue with an aging lead and accumulated continuity, Eon's decision to reboot from the beginning — discarding 20 films of established lore — proved that brand recognition could survive a complete creative restart. Craig went on to appear in four additional Bond films: *Quantum of Solace* (2008), *Skyfall* (2012), *Spectre* (2015), and *No Time to Die* (2021), making his five-film run a complete, arc-driven saga rather than a series of standalone adventures.
